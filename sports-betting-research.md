# Research Project: Information Seeking and Decision-Making in Online Sports Betting

Online sports betting provides a clear setting to examine how people seek information, make financial decisions under uncertainty, and navigate manipulative interface design. Bettors navigate digital environments where sportsbook ads, live odds feeds, social algorithms, and influencer tips interact with mobile platforms to shape financial choices. However, computing research lacks socio-technical frameworks to evaluate how platform interfaces, algorithmic curation, and distributed media jointly influence user risk perception and behavior.

This project examines how young adult bettors (ages 18–24) search for, evaluate, and act on betting information. Focusing on professional and college basketball markets that range from information-dense contexts like the NBA to information-scarce contexts like the WNBA, the study combines three data streams. First, we conduct automated interface audits on popular betting apps’. These include dynamic odds displays, promotional overlays, and time-sensitive prompts during live games. Second, we conduct semi-structured interviews and information horizon mapping with individuals to document where bettors get information, how they evaluate credibility, and how they interpret risk.
